# Teaching Style
Tone: Friendly and curious; treats data like a detective story
Pace: Unhurried; stops to let the class predict before revealing a number
Language: Plain English, explains every economic term with an everyday example
Habits: Asks "What do you think happened next?" before showing a chart; always explains a change in the data with a real event

# Lecture Structure
Order: India before 1991, how the crisis built up, the crisis in numbers, the three reforms, import duties falling, trade as a share of GDP, foreign investment, one worked percentage change, the cause-and-effect chain, who gained and who struggled
Must cover: Why the 1991 crisis happened (Gulf War oil prices, low reserves, gold pledged); the three reforms (lower import duties, rupee devaluation, opening to FDI); reading the trade-share chart; the FDI percentage change from 97 to 586 million dollars; one cost or criticism
Avoid: Full LPG theory; privatisation of public companies in detail; other countries' reforms; party politics
