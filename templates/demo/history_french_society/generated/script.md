Look at Fig. 2 on page 4, and read it from the top. The first estate is the clergy, and the second is the nobility. Both enjoyed privileges simply by birth.

[SECTION title="Who paid which taxes: tithes, taille, indirect taxes and feudal dues"]
Who do you think should pay the state's bills?
[SKETCH_ROW items="box:Tithe | arrow:paid to | result:The Church"]
[SKETCH_ROW items="box:Taille | arrow:paid to | result:The state"]
Let's see who really paid.
[SKETCH_ROW items="box:Salt, tobacco | arrow:taxed on | result:Everyday goods"]
[SKETCH_ROW items="box:Feudal dues | arrow:owed to | result:The lord"]
Here is a sketch of the four payments. Look at the first row: a tithe is a tax paid to the Church. It was one-tenth of the agricultural produce. So a peasant who grew wheat handed one part in ten to the Church.
The second row is the taille. It was a direct tax paid to the state, so the peasant paid the state too.
The third row is indirect taxes. These were levied on everyday things like salt and tobacco, so even buying salt meant paying a tax.
The last row is feudal dues. Peasants owed services to the lord, such as work in his house and fields, army service and building roads.
[SLIDE n=5]
Now the key point.
[SLIDE_HIGHLIGHT n=5 text="The clergy and nobles were exempt from taxes to the state, by birth." while_speaking=true]
The clergy and nobles were exempt from taxes to the state, by birth. The burden of financing the state was borne by the third estate alone. Takeaway: the people with the least land paid the most. Clear?

[SECTION title="The struggle to survive: population growth and rising bread prices"]
Imagine you are a peasant with a family to feed, and bread is your main food. What happens when more mouths need the same bread?
[NOTE_TITLE text="The struggle to survive"]
[NOTE]
- population: about 23 million in 1715, 28 million in 1789
- more people, more demand for grain
- grain could not keep pace, so bread prices rose
- wages did not keep pace with prices (book page 5)
[/NOTE]
Here are the key facts.
[SLIDE n=7]
[SLIDE_HIGHLIGHT n=7 text="The population of France grew from about 23 million in 1715 to 28 million in 1789." while_speaking=true]
The population rose from about 23 million in 1715 to 28 million in 1789. Think of a bakery that bakes the same number of loaves each day, but more customers come every year.
[SLIDE_HIGHLIGHT n=7 text="Grain production could not keep pace with the demand for food." while_speaking=true]
Grain production could not keep pace with the demand, so the price of bread rose rapidly. Most workers were labourers whose wages were fixed by their employers. Those wages did not rise as fast as prices.
[SLIDE_HIGHLIGHT n=7 text="Wages did not keep pace with prices, so the gap between rich and poor widened." while_speaking=true]
So the gap between the poor and the rich widened. Takeaway: more people with the same food means hungry families. Make sense?
